The National Average Drug Acquisition Cost (NADAC) is a public benchmark for what retail community pharmacies pay to buy drugs. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) runs a monthly survey in which pharmacies voluntarily submit their purchase invoices. CMS uses the invoices to estimate an average cost per unit, such as per tablet or per milliliter. An individual pharmacy may pay more or less.

CMS publishes the rates weekly. Each weekly file lists the rate in effect for every package it covers, so the same estimate can appear several weeks in a row. The app stores one price per package from each weekly file it has loaded, dated by the file’s as-of date. See [How current it is](/help/how-current-it-is).

Some state Medicaid programs use NADAC when they set what they pay pharmacies for a drug, and other payers use it as a reference for ingredient costs. NADAC itself isn’t a reimbursement rate, and it doesn’t show what patients pay at the pharmacy.

## Why a package might have no NADAC price

- **Too few purchase prices.** CMS can use invoices for several package NDCs to estimate a rate for a group of drugs. If the survey yields too few usable prices for the group, CMS may be unable to establish a rate.
- **Where the drug is sold.** The survey covers retail community pharmacies. A drug supplied mainly through hospitals or clinics may rarely appear on their invoices.
- **Which drugs qualify.** CMS publishes rates for Medicaid covered outpatient drugs. A package can have an FDA directory listing and fall outside that group. CMS also removes package codes that no longer qualify.

In FDA search, choose **All** under **NADAC price dates** to check the package’s full history in the app. A blank price means the app found no matching NADAC record for that package in the selected period. The weekly files don’t give a reason for an individual missing price.

For the survey rules, see the [CMS NADAC methodology](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid-chip-program-information/by-topics/prescription-drugs/ful-nadac-downloads/nadacmethodology.pdf) and [CMS survey questions and responses](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid-chip-program-information/by-topics/prescription-drugs/ful-nadac-downloads/nadacqa.pdf).
