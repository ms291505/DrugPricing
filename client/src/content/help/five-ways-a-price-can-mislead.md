1. **Check the packages behind an average.** An FDA product’s average can change when different packages have prices in different weeks. Switch to **Packages** to see whether an individual package’s price changed.
2. **Check the pricing unit, strength, and package size.** NADAC reports prices per tablet, milliliter, gram, or other listed unit. Two tablets can have different strengths, and two bottles can contain different amounts. Check those details before comparing prices. For example, Wegovy’s 0.5 mL pens cost more per mL than its 0.75 mL pens, and about the same per pen.
3. **Allow for differences between pharmacies.** NADAC estimates acquisition costs from invoices submitted by participating retail community pharmacies. An individual pharmacy’s acquisition cost may differ from the estimate.
4. **Look for evidence of what caused a change.** A price marker compares two recorded values. Check the package’s history and the source data to investigate the change.
5. **Don’t read an acquisition cost as a patient’s price.** NADAC estimates what the pharmacy paid. A patient’s prescription cost depends on the pharmacy’s price, insurance coverage, and any discounts.
