DrugPricing shows one figure: the published NADAC per unit for a package on a given week. A comparison usually brings in a second figure from somewhere else. Name whose figure each one is before you compare them.

| Figure | Where it comes from | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published NADAC | DrugPricing, from the CMS weekly files | per tablet, mL, gram, or other listed unit |
| Pharmacy purchase price | the pharmacy’s invoice from its wholesaler | usually per package |
| Plan ingredient cost | the plan’s PBM report | per claim, per package, or per unit, depending on the report |

## Put the figures on the same basis

- **The same unit.** Convert your figure to NADAC’s pricing unit. Divide a package price by the units in the package, such as 60 tablets or 3 mL. The package description shows the count or volume. For a per-claim ingredient cost, divide by the quantity dispensed on the claim, in the same unit. A claim can cover part of a package or several packages, so dividing by the package size can give the wrong result.
- **The same package or strength.** Compare the package or product your figure describes. Two strengths, or two package sizes of one strength, can have different NADACs. See [NDCs, products, and packages](/help/ndcs-products-and-packages).
- **The same dates.** Read NADAC for the week your figure covers. For a year-over-year change, read both weeks from the line chart or **View data**.

## What NADAC leaves out

NADAC estimates what retail community pharmacies pay to acquire a drug. CMS excludes discounts and rebates that don’t appear at the drug line-item level on an invoice. Someone with rebate information can see how close a plan’s ingredient cost net of rebates comes to NADAC.

DrugPricing doesn’t import PBM reports or invoices, calculate rebates, or show what a plan or a patient pays. NADAC doesn’t show what patients pay at the pharmacy.
