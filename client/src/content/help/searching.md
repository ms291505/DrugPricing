- Enter at least five characters in a name or description field, or at least five digits
  in **NDC**. Choose **Search** or press Enter.
- A search matches anywhere in the text, regardless of capitalization. For example,
  `loratadine` also matches `desloratadine`.
- If a search returns no matches, check the spelling or try a shorter term of at least five
  characters.
- A search that matches more than 200,000 results shows a message above the results instead
  of a table, because that many are too many to show. In NADAC each price counts as a result.
  In FDA each package counts too, along with its prices, so an FDA search can reach the limit
  with fewer than 200,000 prices. Use a more specific name or description, for example
  `metformin` instead of `tablet`, or shorten the **Dates**. In FDA search you can also pick a
  **Labeler** to narrow the name to one company's products.
- For a brand and its unbranded version in NADAC, search each name separately. For example,
  search `humira` and `adalimumab`.
- **Search** becomes available when you edit the terms or can retry after an error. Dates and
  filters apply automatically.
- **Clear search** empties the search fields and puts the dates and filters back to their
  defaults, so you can start over. Pinned items stay.
- Under **Search**, your searches and shared pins stay in this browser tab. To keep a copy
  between visits, choose **Add search to workspace**, which opens it in a new workspace tab.

## Finding a company's products

In FDA search, **Labeler** narrows a brand or generic name search to the companies you pick.
It can't search on its own: enter a name too. For example, `chloride` matches too many products
to show, but `chloride` with one company can fit. Type part of a company's name in **Labeler**
and pick it from the list. Spelling doesn't have to be exact: `pfeizer` offers Pfizer under
**Did you mean**. You can pick several labelers. Like **Dates**, picks apply at once, without
**Search**. If a name matches too many results to show, picking a labeler can bring it under
the limit. The list offers the labelers whose products the page would
show, so check **OTC drugs** or **Results without prices** to see more. **Can't find a
company?** under the field opens this section.

A labeler is the company that registered the NDC (see the [glossary](/help/glossary)), which
isn't always the company you know. A parent company's products may be listed under a
subsidiary's name. For example, Bristol Myers Squibb's Eliquis is listed under E.R. Squibb &
Sons, so typing `bristol` finds no labeler. To find the right one:

1. Search the brand name, such as `eliquis`.
2. Read the **Labeler** column in the results.
3. Pick that labeler: the results narrow to it at once. Search a related name, such as the
   generic name, to see what else that labeler lists.

Typing a parent company's name also finds labelers that name it, such as Wyeth Pharmaceuticals
LLC, a subsidiary of Pfizer Inc.
